# 5.5 System Schematic and Wiring Diagrams

The physical realization of a multi-sensor edge node depends not only on firmware logic but also on the correctness and precision of the underlying hardware connections. A well-documented schematic captures every electrical interface—power rails, signal paths, communication buses, and ground returns—and serves as the authoritative reference during assembly, debugging, and peer review. This section presents the complete wiring architecture of the autonomous fire detection node, covering pin assignments, bus topologies, and signal-conditioning requirements for each of the five sensing modalities integrated around the Arduino UNO R4 WiFi (Renesas RA4M1 + ESP32-S3).

---

## 5.5.1 Pin Allocation Strategy

The Arduino UNO R4 WiFi exposes the full peripheral set of the Renesas RA4M1 microcontroller, which integrates a 14-bit successive-approximation ADC capable of addressing up to 25 analog input channels, together with hardware I²C, SPI, and UART controllers (Renesas Electronics Corporation, 2023). Given that the five sensors on the node present both analog resistive outputs and a digital I²C interface, the pin allocation was designed to achieve strict electrical isolation between the analog and digital domains and to keep all ADC-channel assignments within the RA4M1's main analog port group (A0–A5).

Table 5.1 summarises the pin allocation for each sensor module. The three MEMS gas sensors—smoke (particulate analogue), VOC, and CO—each present a ratiometric resistive output whose voltage is read on dedicated ADC channels A0, A1, and A2, respectively. The IR flame sensor provides both an analog intensity output (A3) and a threshold-based digital output (D2), the latter routed to an external-interrupt-capable GPIO so that a hardware interrupt can be raised on flame onset without polling overhead. The AHT20 Temperature/Humidity sensor communicates exclusively over the two-wire I²C bus on the RA4M1's hardware SDA/SCL lines (A4/A5 in Arduino UNO form-factor nomenclature), which are pulled up to 3.3 V through 4.7 kΩ resistors.

| ------------------ | ----------------- | ------------------ | -------------- |
| MEMS Smoke         | Analog resistive  | A0                 | ADC (0–5 V)    |
| MEMS VOC           | Analog resistive  | A1                 | ADC (0–5 V)    |
| MEMS CO            | Analog resistive  | A2                 | ADC (0–5 V)    |
| IR Flame (analog)  | Analog voltage    | A3                 | ADC (0–5 V)    |
| IR Flame (digital) | Digital threshold | D2                 | GPIO INT       |

## 5.5.2 Analog Signal Chain and Conditioning

Each MEMS gas sensor (smoke, VOC, CO) operates on the chemiresistive principle: the sensitive element's resistance, \(R*S\), changes in the presence of the target analyte, and this change is converted to a voltage by an on-module voltage-divider load resistor \(R_L\). The resulting output voltage \(V*{out}\) is expressed as:

\[V*{out} = V*{CC} \cdot \frac{R_L}{R_S + R_L}\]

where \(V*{CC} = 5\) V is the heater and divider supply rail. This ratiometric signal is fed directly to the RA4M1's 14-bit ADC input pins, which accept rail-to-rail signals up to \(V*{CC}\). Ren et al. (2023) demonstrate that MEMS gas sensor interface circuits based on SAR ADC technology with a variable-gain pre-amplifier and a low-pass filter cascade can resolve resistance changes spanning four orders of magnitude (100 Ω – 1 MΩ) with conversion errors below 1%, confirming that the on-chip 14-bit SAR ADC of the RA4M1 is well-suited to the resistive range of the DFRobot Fermion sensor family (Ren et al., 2023). To suppress aliasing and high-frequency switching noise generated by the sensor heating elements, a first-order RC low-pass filter (R = 1 kΩ, C = 100 nF, \(f_c \approx 1.6\) kHz) is placed on each analog line between the sensor module output and the MCU pin. This cut-off is chosen to be well above the 10 Hz sampling rate of the inference pipeline while attenuating noise introduced by the MEMS heater drive circuitry.

The IR flame sensor (DFRobot Gravity Analog, spectral sensitivity 760–1100 nm) outputs a continuous 0–5 V analog signal proportional to incident infrared irradiance, supplemented by a comparator-driven digital output. The digital threshold line is connected to D2 (INT0), enabling a hardware interrupt-driven pre-screening stage that can assert a provisional alert independently of the TinyML inference cycle.

## 5.5.3 I²C Bus Implementation for the AHT20 Sensor

The AHT20 temperature and humidity sensor communicates via the I²C protocol at a fixed 7-bit address (0x38), supporting standard (100 kHz) and fast (400 kHz) bus speeds (ASAIR / Aosong Electronics, 2020). The node operates the I²C bus at 400 kHz (fast mode) to minimize the blocking window during each AHT20 read cycle, which comprises a trigger-measurement command, a 80 ms conversion delay, and a 6-byte read transaction. The SDA and SCL lines are each pulled to the 3.3 V rail—provided by the RA4M1's on-board LDO regulator—through 4.7 kΩ pull-up resistors, a value selected to satisfy the I²C specification's maximum rise-time constraint of 300 ns at 400 kHz with the estimated bus capacitance of approximately 40 pF.

Multi-sensor systems that combine analog chemiresistive sensors with I²C digital sensors on a shared microcontroller have been demonstrated in early fire detection deployments: Vorwerk et al. (2024) describe a sensor node architecture integrating VOC (SGP40), CO, H₂, CO₂, particulate matter, and relative humidity (SHT4x) sensors on a single ESP32 board using a combination of analog and I²C interfaces, collecting data at 1 sample per 10 seconds without bus contention or timing conflicts, which validates the architectural approach adopted in the present node (Vorwerk et al., 2024).

## 5.5.4 Power Rail Architecture and Decoupling

The node is powered from a 5 V USB supply or regulated wall adapter. The MEMS gas sensor heater elements each draw approximately 150–180 mA at steady-state, making rail decoupling critical. A 100 µF electrolytic bulk capacitor is placed at the shared 5 V bus entry point to absorb heater inrush transients, supplemented by 100 nF ceramic decoupling capacitors placed within 5 mm of each sensor's VCC pin. The AHT20 is powered from the RA4M1's 3.3 V LDO output, which is rated at 500 mA—well above the sensor's ≤200 µA quiescent current.

Because the RA4M1 ADC reference is tied to AVCC (5 V), all analog sensor modules are also powered at 5 V to maintain the ratiometric accuracy described in Section 5.5.2. The IR flame sensor module similarly operates at 5 V. Shared ground routing follows a star topology from a single ground plane tie-point at the Arduino GND pin, minimizing ground-loop currents that could corrupt low-level analog readings. Effective decoupling strategies for multi-sensor IoT fire detection hardware are consistently emphasized in embedded hardware design literature: Yun et al. (2022) note that inadequate power-rail decoupling in multi-sensor fire nodes is a primary cause of elevated ADC noise floors and spurious sensor readings in operational deployments (Yun et al., 2022).

## 5.5.5 Complete System Schematic Description

The full node schematic is organized around the Arduino UNO R4 WiFi as the central hub, with the five sensor modules arranged in a logical star topology. The MEMS smoke, VOC, and CO modules each present a standard three-wire interface (VCC, GND, AOUT) connected to the 5 V rail, common ground, and ADC pins A0–A2, respectively. The IR flame sensor presents a four-wire interface (VCC, GND, AOUT, DOUT); AOUT is wired to A3 and DOUT is wired to D2 via a 10 kΩ pull-up to 3.3 V to ensure safe logic levels at the RA4M1 GPIO. The AHT20 breakout board presents a four-wire interface (VCC, GND, SDA, SCL) connected to the 3.3 V rail, common ground, and the hardware I²C pins.

An active buzzer (5 V, ≤30 mA) and a red LED indicator (470 Ω series resistor, ~10 mA) are driven from GPIO push-pull outputs D8 and D9, respectively, through 2N2222 NPN transistors in a low-side switch configuration to ensure the RA4M1's GPIO source current limits are not exceeded. The complete wiring reference is formalized in Appendix B (Wiring Diagrams & PCB Layouts), which contains the KiCad schematic file and the bill of materials. The node architecture follows the design principles identified in multi-sensor fire detection research, where a clear separation of power domains, dedicated analog-ground planes, and hardware-interrupt-capable flame sensor connections are recommended for reliable low-latency detection (Vorwerk et al., 2024; Ren et al., 2023).
