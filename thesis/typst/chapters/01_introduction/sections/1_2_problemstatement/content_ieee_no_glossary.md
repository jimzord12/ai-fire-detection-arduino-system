The pervasive threat of fire necessitates robust and reliable detection systems. Traditional fire detection technologies, while foundational, frequently suffer from significant limitations, particularly in their susceptibility to false alarms. These systems often rely on single-sensor thresholds (e.g., smoke or heat), leading to ambiguous interpretations of environmental conditions [1]. For instance, cooking fumes, steam from bathrooms, or dust can trigger conventional smoke detectors, resulting in costly evacuations, operational disruptions, and a reduction in user trust and vigilance towards actual fire incidents [2]. This issue is exacerbated in complex, dynamic environments where environmental variables fluctuate widely, making binary fire/no-fire classifications inadequate for precise risk assessment.

The current paradigm often fails to differentiate between genuine fire events, normal environmental conditions, and common false alarm triggers. This lack of a nuanced classification capability poses a substantial problem for the deployment of autonomous safety systems, as false positives undermine system credibility and efficiency. The inability of existing systems to accurately discern between these three critical states (fire, no-fire, and false alarm) leads to preventable economic losses and, more critically, a diminished safety posture due to a high incidence of non-threatening alarms [3]. There is a pressing need for intelligent edge-deployed solutions that can leverage multi-sensor data to perform a more sophisticated, multi-class classification, thereby reducing false alarms and improving the overall effectiveness of fire detection mechanisms [4].

The scope of this work is limited to stationary Autonomous Sensing Nodes. While mobile robotic platforms offer flexibility, their power constraints, navigational complexities, and high computational demands introduce challenges beyond the immediate focus of developing an intelligent, localized detection unit [5]. This thesis specifically addresses the challenges within a fixed-point monitoring context, aiming to demonstrate the efficacy of sensor fusion and TinyML for nuanced fire detection in such a stationary deployment. Future work may explore the extension of these principles to mobile platforms, but for the present, the focus remains on optimizing performance and reliability within a static framework.
